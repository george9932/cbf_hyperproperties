\begin{abstract}
    Many requirements in cyber-physical systems are inherently relational, capturing dependencies across multiple system executions. Such requirements, known as hyperproperties, cannot be expressed using standard temporal logics. 
    This paper presents an online control synthesis framework for hyperproperties of continuous-time control-affine systems using control barrier functions (CBFs). Hyperproperties are specified in HyperLTL and enforced by constructing CBFs over an augmented state space that captures multiple system trajectories. The resulting conditions are added as constraints into a quadratic program, enabling real-time controller synthesis with formal guarantees. 
    As a representative example, we introduce a notion of approximate opacity for continuous-time systems and synthesize controllers that enforce it. The effectiveness of the proposed approach is demonstrated through a case study, showing that the framework can simultaneously achieve task satisfaction and security requirements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autonomous cyber-physical systems (CPS) increasingly operate in open environments where correct behavior must be ensured not only along a single trajectory, but across families of trajectories arising from uncertainty and adversarial observation. At the same time, modern robotic systems must accomplish complex tasks while providing formal correctness guarantees. Formal specification and synthesis methods based on temporal logics have therefore become central tools for motion planning and control \cite{fainekos2009, kloetzer2008}, enabling high-level task specification, and fully-automated and correct-by-design controller synthesis \cite{smith2011}.

A fundamental limitation of standard temporal logics is that they specify requirements over individual executions. However, many requirements in robotics and CPS are inherently relational, i.e., they capture relationships among multiple system executions \cite{wang2020}. Examples include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item information-flow security properties \cite{zhao2024} such as \emph{opacity} \cite{saboori2007}, which require that an external observer cannot infer whether the system has executed a secret behavior based on available observations,
    \item \emph{contraction} \cite{lohmiller1998}, which requires that all trajectories initialized within a prescribed neighborhood of a nominal trajectory converge towards it, and
    \item \emph{optimality} which requires finding trajectories that minimize a given performance criterion, such as time or control effort.
\end{enumerate}
Such multi-execution requirements cannot, in general, be expressed in classic temporal logics, motivating the introduction of hyperproperties \cite{clarkson2010}. Hyperlogics such as HyperLTL and HyperCTL extend temporal logics with explicit quantification over traces \cite{clarkson2014}, enabling formulas with alternating universal ($\forall$) and existential ($\exists$) quantifiers \cite{finkbeiner2015}. For continuous-space CPS, HyperSTL extends signal temporal logic to express hyperproperties over real-valued trajectories \cite{nguyen2017}.

Verification of hyperproperties has advanced substantially via automata-based model checking \cite{clarkson2014}, dedicated tools \cite{finkbeiner2015}, and, more recently, extensions to discrete-time dynamical systems with certificate-based approaches \cite{anand2024}. In contrast, control synthesis for hyperproperties has received increasing attention only recently. In both verification and control synthesis, most existing methods target discrete-event systems (DES) \cite{wang2020, finkbeiner2015}, while recent approaches extend to discrete-time dynamical systems \cite{anand2024, zhao2025}, leaving continuous-time systems largely unexplored. A similar limitation arises in opacity-preserving control synthesis, where existing approaches are primarily developed for DES \cite{yang2023, yu2022} or for discrete-time systems with offline synthesis \cite{zhong2025}.

However, CPS require online, real-time control capable of reacting to disturbances and uncertainty during operation. A growing body of work in robotics employs control barrier functions (CBFs), which provide an optimization-based framework for online synthesis of safety-critical controllers by rendering a specified safe set forward invariant \cite{ames2014, ames2019}. Moreover, CBF-based methods have been integrated with temporal-logic objectives to enable real-time control for complex tasks \cite{srinivasan2021, lindemann2019}.

In many task-planning approaches in the literature, executions associated with universal quantifiers are not generated by the synthesized controller, but instead range over all admissible system behaviors \cite{wang2020, zhao2025}. In this case, the controller must satisfy the specification against every possible execution, effectively reducing the problem to verification. This setting is natural for a class of hyperproperties that capture intrinsic system properties, such as optimality \cite{wang2020}, incremental stability \cite{angeli2002}, and diagnosability \cite{zhao2024}, where the hyperproperty must hold for all possible executions associated with universal quantifiers.

A second class of hyperproperties captures robustness with respect to uncertainty, e.g., in initial conditions or disturbances, and is typically of the form $\exists \pi \, \forall \pi'. \, \varphi_1(\pi,\pi')$. Here, $\pi$ is a nominal trajectory and $\pi'$ ranges over trajectories induced by uncertainty. The goal is to design a nominal controller based solely on the nominal state such that the specification holds uniformly for all admissible perturbations. Finally, there are hyperproperties where all executions can be designed. A representative example is opacity, expressed as $\forall \pi \, \exists \pi'. \, \varphi_2(\pi,\pi')$, where $\pi$ denotes the real execution and $\pi'$ an alternative one. In this setting, the controllers for both trajectories are jointly generated using the augmented state. Consequently, control inputs can depend on both states. From a control perspective, the key distinction between these classes lies in whether the universally quantified executions can be controlled and used for feedback: (i) in the first class, all executions are exogenous; (ii) in the second, all executions are controlled, but we need to account for all possible states of $\pi'$ to design the nominal controller; and (iii) in the third class, all executions are controlled and can be jointly synthesized.

This paper bridges hyperproperty specifications and online CBF-based control for continuous-time control-affine systems. The proposed framework enables real-time satisfaction of hyperproperties and can be seamlessly combined with CBFs derived from temporal-logic specifications \cite{srinivasan2021, lindemann2019}. Theoretical guarantees and an illustrative case study demonstrate the approach. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} introduces preliminaries and notation, and formulates the control problem. Section~\ref{sec:control_synthesis} presents the control synthesis framework and introduces approximate opacity as an example.
Section~\ref{sec:case_study} provides a realistic case study, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.

\section{Preliminaries} \label{sec:preliminaries}

\textbf{Notation.} Let $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Z}$, and $\mathbb{N}$ denote the sets of real numbers, integers and positive integers, respectively, and let $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denote the set of nonnegative real numbers. For any $n, m \in \mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{R}^n$ denotes the space of $n$-dimensional column vectors and $\mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ the space of $n \times m$ real matrices. Vectors are denoted by bold lowercase letters and matrices by bold uppercase letters. The vectors of all zeros and all ones are denoted by $\mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{1}$, respectively. For any $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 & \dots & x_n \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\|\mathbf{x}\|$ denotes the Euclidean norm, i.e., $\|\mathbf{x}\| = \sqrt{\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{x}}$, and $\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty$ denotes the infinity norm, i.e., $\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty = \max_i{|x_i|}$. The notation $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots)$ denotes a diagonal matrix with entries $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots$, and $\operatorname{blkdiag}(\mathbf{B}_1, \mathbf{B}_2, \dots)$ denotes a block diagonal matrix with blocks $\mathbf{B}_1, \mathbf{B}_2, \dots$, where $\mathbf{B}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ for all $i \in \mathbb{N}$. A continuous function $\alpha: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is of class-$\mathcal{K}$ if it is strictly increasing, and with $\alpha(0) = 0$. A function $\beta: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \times \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is of class-$\mathcal{K} \mathcal{L}$ if (i) for each fixed $s$, $\alpha(r, s)$ is class-$\mathcal{K}$, and (ii) for each fixed $r$, $\alpha(r, s)$ is decreasing in $s$ and with $\lim_{s \to \infty} \alpha(r, s) = 0$. Given a continuously differentiable function $V : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ and a vector field $\mathbf{f} : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$, the Lie derivative of $V$ along $\mathbf{f}$ is defined as $L_\mathbf{f} V(\mathbf{x}) = \tfrac{\partial V}{\partial \mathbf{x}} \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})$. 

\subsection{System model}

We consider a nonlinear input-affine control system
\begin{equation} \label{eq:system}
    S: \quad \dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}) \, \mathbf{u}, \quad \mathbf{x}(0) = \mathbf{x}_0.
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ denotes the state, $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{U} \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ the control input. The initial state $\mathbf{x}_0$ belongs to $\mathbb{X}_0 \subseteq \mathbb{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$. The functions $\mathbf{f}: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathbf{g}: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ are locally Lipschitz continuous.

Given a controller $\boldsymbol{\nu}(t): \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \rightarrow \mathbb{U}$ and an initial state $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{X}_0$, we denote by $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(t): \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \to \mathbb{X}$ the corresponding closed-loop trajectory.

\subsection{Control barrier functions}

Consider a system as in \eqref{eq:system} and a set $\mathcal{C}$, which is defined as the superlevel set of a continuously differentiable function $b: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$, i.e., 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:safe_set}
    \mathcal{C} := \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X} \mid b(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0 \}.
\end{equation}  

\begin{definition}\label{def:cbf}
    Consider a set defined as in \eqref{eq:safe_set}. The function $b(\mathbf{x})$ is a CBF if there exists a locally Lipschitz class-$\mathcal{K}$ function $\alpha$ such that 
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:cbf}
       \sup_{\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{U}} \Big[ L_\mathbf{f} b ( \mathbf{x}) + L_\mathbf{g} b ( \mathbf{x}) \, \mathbf{u} \Big] \ge -\alpha(b(\mathbf{x})), \ \forall \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X}.
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:forward_invariance}
    \cite{ames2019} Let $\mathcal{C} \subset \mathbb{X}$ be defined as the superlevel set of a continuously differentiable function $b: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. If $b$ is a CBF, then any Lipschitz continuous controller $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})$ such that $L_\mathbf{f} b ( \mathbf{x}) + L_\mathbf{g} b ( \mathbf{x}) \, \mathbf{u} \!\ge\! -\alpha(b(\mathbf{x}))$ renders $\mathcal{C}$ forward invariant for the system \eqref{eq:system}, i.e., $  \mathbf{x}(0) \in \mathcal{C} \Rightarrow \mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathcal{C} $ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$.
\end{theorem}

Next, we introduce finite-time convergence control barrier functions (FCBFs), which are used to guarantee reachability of a set in finite time. Moreover, FCBFs provide an explicit upper bound on the convergence time (cf. Theorem~\ref{thm:reachability}).
\begin{definition} \label{def:fcbf}
    Consider a set defined as in \eqref{eq:safe_set}. The function $b(\mathbf{x})$ is an FCBF if there exists $\rho \in [0,1)$ and $\gamma > 0$ such that, for all $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X}$,
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:fcbf}
       \sup_{\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{U}} \Big[ L_\mathbf{f} b ( \mathbf{x}) + L_\mathbf{g} b ( \mathbf{x}) \, \mathbf{u} + \gamma \, \operatorname{sign} \big( b(\mathbf{x}) \big) \big| b(\mathbf{x}) \big|^\rho \Big] \ge 0.
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:reachability}
    \cite{li2018} Let $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X} \mid b(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0 \}$, where $b$ is a continuously differentiable FCBF. For any initial condition $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{X}$ and any continuous controller $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})$ such that $L_\mathbf{f} b ( \mathbf{x}) + L_\mathbf{g} b ( \mathbf{x}) \, \mathbf{u} + \gamma \, \operatorname{sign} \big( b(\mathbf{x}) \big) \big| b(\mathbf{x}) \big|^\rho \ge 0$, it holds that $\mathbf{x}(T) \in \mathcal{C}$, where $T = \tfrac{|b(\mathbf{x}(0)|^{1 - \rho}}{\gamma (1 - \rho)}$. Moreover, $\mathcal{C}$ is forward invariant for the system \eqref{eq:system}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Linear temporal logic}

Let $\mathcal{AP}$ be a finite set of atomic propositions. The syntax of LTL is defined recursively \cite{baier2008}:
\begin{equation*} \label{eq:ltl}
    \varphi \Coloneqq \pi \mid \lnot \, \varphi \mid \varphi_1 \land \varphi_2 \mid \varphi_1 \, \mathcal{U} \, \varphi_2,
\end{equation*}
where $\pi \in \mathcal{AP}$, and $\varphi$, $\varphi_1$, $\varphi_2$ are LTL formulas, and $\mathcal{U}$ denotes the \emph{until} operator. The disjunction ($\lor$) is derived as $\varphi_1 \lor \varphi_2 := \lnot (\lnot \varphi_1 \land \lnot \varphi_2)$, the Boolean $true$ as $\pi \lor \lnot \pi$, while the temporal operators \emph{eventually} ($\lozenge$) and \emph{always} ($\square$) are defined as $\lozenge \varphi  := true \ \mathcal{U} \, \varphi$, and $\square \, \varphi {:=} \lnot  \lozenge \, \lnot \varphi$, respectively. The $\emph{next}$ ($\mathcal{X}$) operator is omitted since it is not meaningful for continuous-time systems.

A trace is an infinite word $\sigma = \sigma_0 \sigma_1 \dots$ with $\sigma_i \in 2^\mathcal{AP}$ for all $i \in \mathbb{N}$. The set of all traces is $\Sigma^\omega = (2^\mathcal{AP})^\omega$. LTL formulas are interpreted over infinite traces. Satisfaction of an LTL formula over a trace $\sigma$, denoted $\sigma \models \varphi$, is defined according to the standard semantics \cite{baier2008}.

\subsection{HyperLTL} \label{subsec:hyperltl}
HyperLTL extends LTL with quantification over multiple traces. Its syntax is \cite{clarkson2014}:
\begin{equation*} \label{eq:hyperltl}
    \begin{aligned}
        \psi &\Coloneqq \exists \tau. \psi \mid \forall \tau .  \psi \mid \varphi' \\
        \varphi' &\Coloneqq \pi_\tau \mid \lnot \, \varphi \mid \varphi_1 \land \varphi_2 \mid \varphi_1 \, \mathcal{U} \, \varphi_2,
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where $\tau$ is a trace variable and $\pi_\tau$ denotes that proposition $\pi \in \mathcal{AP}$ holds on trace $\tau$. Let $T$ be an infinite set of trace variables and $\mathbb{S} \subseteq \Sigma^\omega$ a set of traces. A trace assignment is a function $V: T \to \mathbb{S}$. For $\sigma \in \mathbb{S}$, we denote by $V[\tau \mapsto \sigma]$ the assignment that maps $\tau$ to $\sigma$ and agrees with $V$ on all other variables. For $j \in \mathbb{N}$, we define $V[j, \infty]$ as the assignment $V(\tau)[j] \, V(\tau)[j+1] \dots$ for all $\tau \in T$. Given a trace assignment $V$, satisfaction of a HyperLTL formula $\psi$, denoted by $V \models \psi$, is \cite{clarkson2014}:
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        &V \models \pi_\tau &\Leftrightarrow& \ \pi \in V(\tau)[0] \\
        &V \models \neg \varphi &\Leftrightarrow& \ V \not\models \varphi \\
        &V \models \varphi_1 \land \varphi_2 &\Leftrightarrow&  \ V \models \varphi_1 \text{ and }  V \models \varphi_2 \\
        &V \models \varphi_1 \mathcal{U} \varphi_2 &\Leftrightarrow& \ \text{there exists } i \text{ such that } V[i, \infty] \models \varphi_2 \\
        & & & \text{ and for all } j < i, \  V[j, \infty] \models \varphi_1 \\
        &V \models \exists \tau. \psi &\Leftrightarrow& \ \text{there exists } \sigma \in \mathbb{S}: V[\tau \mapsto \sigma] \models \psi \\
        &V \models \forall \tau. \psi &\Leftrightarrow& \ \text{for all } \sigma \in \mathbb{S}:  V[\tau \mapsto \sigma] \models \psi.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The empty trace assignment is denoted by $V_\emptyset$. If $V_\emptyset \models \varphi$, then $\mathbb{S}$ satisfies a formula $\varphi$, i.e., $\mathbb{S} \models \varphi$.

Each atomic proposition $\pi \in \mathcal{AP}$ is associated with a continuously differentiable function $b_\pi: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$. The proposition $\pi$ holds at a state $\mathbf{x}$ if and only if $b_\pi(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0$, i.e., on the superlevel set $\{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X} \mid b_\pi(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0 \}$. While LTL and HyperLTL are originally defined for DES, traces for continuous systems can be induced from trajectories. Following Def.~4 in \cite{kloetzer2008}, the trace assignment $V$ can be extended to trajectories; given an initial condition $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{X}_0$ and a controller $\boldsymbol{\nu}(t)$, $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, we denote by $V(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\nu}}) = \sigma = \sigma[1] \sigma[2] \dots \in \Sigma^\omega$ the trace induced by the trajectory $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(t)$. The trace $\sigma$ represents the set of propositions satisfied along the trajectory. If a set $\mathbb{S} \subseteq \Sigma^\omega$ is the set of traces generated by a system $S$ from \eqref{eq:system} and $\mathbb{S}$ satisfies a HyperLTL formula $\psi$, we write $S \models \varphi$.

HyperLTL formulas are written as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:hyperltl_formula}
    \psi = \mu_1 \tau_1 \, \mu_2 \tau_2 \dots \mu_p \tau_p. \, \varphi(\tau_1, \tau_2, \dots, \tau_p), 
\end{equation}
where $\mu_i \in \{\forall, \exists\}$ and $\varphi$ is an LTL formula. In this work, we consider formulas with $\varphi = \square \, \phi_1 \land \lozenge \, \phi_2$. Each $\phi_i$, $i = 1,2$, is given by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:hyperltl_subformula}
    \phi_i = \bigvee_{k=1}^K \, \bigwedge_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} \pi_j,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_k$, $k = \{1, \dots, K\}$, are index sets.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $p$ denote the number of trace variables in HyperLTL formulas in \eqref{eq:hyperltl_formula}. We form the $p$-fold self-composition \cite{anand2024} of the system \eqref{eq:system} by introducing the augmented state and input spaces $\bar{\mathbb{X}} \coloneqq \mathbb{X} \times \dots \times \mathbb{X} $ and $\bar{\mathbb{U}} \coloneqq \mathbb{U} \times \dots \times \mathbb{U}$, with augmented state and input
\begin{equation*}
    \bar{\mathbf{x}} = (\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_p) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}_1^\top & \dots & \mathbf{x}_p^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \bar{\mathbb{X}}, \text{ and}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
    \bar{\mathbf{u}} = (\mathbf{u}, \dots, \mathbf{u}_p) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{u}_1^\top & \dots & \mathbf{u}_p^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \bar{\mathbb{U}}.
\end{equation*}
The augmented dynamics are $\dot{\bar{\mathbf{x}}} = \bar{\mathbf{f}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) + \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \, \bar{\mathbf{u}}$, where 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:augmented_system}
    \begin{aligned}
        \bar{\mathbf{f}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) &= \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_1)^\top & \dots & \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_p)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top, \text{ and} \\ 
        \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) &= \operatorname{blkdiag} (\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_1), \dots, \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_p)).
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Each trajectory $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, i}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_i}(t)$, $i \in \{1, \dots, p\}$, corresponds to a copy of $S$ with initial condition $\mathbf{x}_{0, i} \in \mathbb{X}_0$ and controller $\boldsymbol{\nu}_i: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \rightarrow \mathbb{U}$. We denote the augmented trajectory as $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0, \bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}} = (\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, 1}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_1}, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, p}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_p})$. Each trace variable $\tau_i$ in \eqref{eq:hyperltl_formula} corresponds to $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, i}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_i}$ and is mapped to a trace $\sigma_i$ by the trace assignment function $V(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, i}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_i}) = \sigma_i$.

\begin{problem} \label{pr:HyperLTL}
    Consider $p$ copies of $S$ from \eqref{eq:system} with initial conditions $\mathbf{x}_{0, i} \in \mathbb{X}_0$, $i = 1, \dots, p$, and a HyperLTL formula $\psi$ of the form \eqref{eq:hyperltl_formula}. Synthesize controllers $\boldsymbol{\nu}_i: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \rightarrow \mathbb{U}$, $i = 1, \dots, p$, such that the augmented trajectory $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0, \bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}} = (\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, 1}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_1}, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, p}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_p})$ satisfies $\psi$, i.e., $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0, \bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}} \models \psi$. 
\end{problem}

\section{Control synthesis for hyperproperties} \label{sec:control_synthesis}

In this section, we present an online controller synthesis methodology for HyperLTL specifications of the form \eqref{eq:hyperltl_formula}, based on the construction of CBFs and FCBFs in the augmented state space, and their enforcement via a quadratic program (QP).

We classify HyperLTL formulas according to the structure of their quantifier prefix. A formula is called a $\forall^\star$-HyperLTL formula if $\mu_i = \forall$ for all $i = 1, \dots, p$, and an $\exists^\star$-HyperLTL formula if $\mu_i = \exists$ for all $i$. In general, given a quantifier sequence $\mu_1 \dots \mu_p$, we refer to the corresponding fragment of HyperLTL by grouping consecutive quantifiers of the same type. For instance, a formula with prefix $\forall \dots \forall \, \exists \dots \exists$ is of fragment $\forall^\star \exists^\star$, while with prefix $\exists \dots \exists \, \forall \dots \forall \, \exists \dots \exists$ is of fragment $\exists^\star \, \forall^\star \, \exists^\star$.

\subsection{$\forall^\star$-HyperLTL formulas} \label{subsec:forall}

This case constitutes a direct extension of LTL satisfaction to the augmented state space. Consider a $\forall^\star$-HyperLTL formula $\psi = \forall \tau_1 \dots \forall \tau_p. \, \varphi(\tau_1, \dots, \tau_p)$. By considering the self-composed (augmented) system, satisfaction of $\psi$ reduces to verifying $\varphi$ over the augmented trajectory $\bar{\tau} = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0, \bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}$, which we denote compactly as $\forall \bar{\tau}. \, \varphi(\bar{\tau})$. According to the HyperLTL semantics (cf. Section~\ref{subsec:hyperltl}), this requires that $V \models \varphi$ for all traces $\sigma_i$ induced by the trajectories $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, i}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_i}$, i.e., $V(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_{0, i}, \boldsymbol{\nu}_i}) = \sigma_i$ for all $i$.

\textbf{Case~1.} Consider the case $\varphi = \square \, \phi_1$, where $\phi_1$ is given in \eqref{eq:hyperltl_subformula}. Each atomic proposition $\pi_j$ is associated with a continuously differentiable function $b_{\pi_j} : \bar{\mathbb{X}} \to \mathbb{R}$, such that $\pi_j$ holds if $b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0$. For each $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, the conjunction term $\bigwedge_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} \pi_j$ requires that all predicates $\pi_j$, $j \in \mathcal{I}_k$, hold simultaneously, i.e., $b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t)) \ge 0$, for all $j \in \mathcal{I}_k$. This is equivalent to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:min_j}
    \min_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t)) \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Since $\phi_1$ is a disjunction of such conjunctions, satisfaction of $\phi_1$ requires that there exists $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ such that \eqref{eq:min_j} holds, i.e.,
\[
    \max_{k=1,\dots,K} \, \min_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t)) \ge 0, \quad \forall t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}.
\]

However, both the $\min$ and $\max$ operators are non-differentiable. To enable CBF-based synthesis, we construct smooth under-approximations using the Log-Sum-Exp function. For each $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, we define
\begin{equation} \label{eq:smooth_min}
    m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) := -\frac{1}{\beta'} \log \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} e^{-\beta' b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})} \right),
\end{equation}
with $\beta' > 0$. By standard bounds on the Log-Sum-Exp function, $m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \le \min_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$, and thus enforcing $m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0$ guarantees satisfaction of $\bigwedge_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} \pi_j$. Next, we define
\begin{equation} \label{eq:smooth_max}
    b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) := \frac{1}{\beta} \log \left( \sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}})} \right) - \frac{\log K}{\beta},
\end{equation}
with $\beta > 0$. By construction, $b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \le \max_k m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$, and thus enforcing $b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0$ guarantees that at least one conjunction is satisfied, i.e., $\phi_1$ holds. We define the set
\begin{equation} \label{eq:augmented_safe_set}
    \mathcal{C} := \big\{ \bar{\mathbf{x}} \in \bar{\mathbb{X}} \mid b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0 \big\}. 
\end{equation}
The problem reduces to rendering $\mathcal{C}$ forward invariant for the augmented dynamics in \eqref{eq:augmented_system}. Forward invariance of $\mathcal{C}$ ensures that $\phi_1$ holds for all time, and hence $\psi$ is satisfied.
\begin{theorem} \label{thm:safety_hyper}
    Consider the augmented system in \eqref{eq:augmented_system} and a $\forall^\star$-HyperLTL formula $\psi = \forall \bar{\tau} . \, \square \, \phi_1$, where $\phi_1$ is given by \eqref{eq:hyperltl_subformula}. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be given by \eqref{eq:augmented_safe_set}. Suppose that $b$ is a CBF according to Def.~\ref{def:cbf}. If there exists a Lipschitz continuous controller $\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t) = \bar{\mathbf{u}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t))$ satisfying the CBF condition in \eqref{eq:cbf} for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, then for all initial conditions $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0 \in \mathcal{C}$, the induced trace assignment $V$ satisfies $\psi$, i.e., $V \models \psi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:forward_invariance}, the set $\mathcal{C}$ is forward invariant. Hence, for any $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0 \in \mathcal{C}$ and all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, we have $b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t)) \ge 0$, which implies that $\phi_1$ holds for all time. Therefore, $V \models \psi$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Case~2.} We now consider $\varphi(\bar{\tau}) = \lozenge \, \phi_2$, where $\phi_2$ is given by \eqref{eq:hyperltl_subformula}. Satisfaction of $\varphi$ requires that there exists $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ such that $\phi_2$ holds. 

For each $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, let $m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$ and $b(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$ be defined as in \eqref{eq:smooth_min} and \eqref{eq:smooth_max}, respectively. Then, we have $m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \le \min_{j \in \mathcal{I}_k} b_{\pi_j}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$ and $b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \le \max_k m_k(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$. Consequently, $b(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0$ guarantees that $\phi_2$ is satisfied.
\begin{theorem} \label{thm:reachability_hyper}
    Consider the augmented system in \eqref{eq:augmented_system} and a $\forall^\star$-HyperLTL formula $\psi = \forall \bar{\tau} . \, \lozenge \, \phi_2$, where $\phi_2$ is given by \eqref{eq:hyperltl_subformula}. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be given by \eqref{eq:augmented_safe_set}. Suppose that $b$ is an FCBF according to Def.~\ref{def:fcbf}. If there exists a continuous controller $\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t) = \bar{\mathbf{u}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t))$ satisfying the FCBF condition in \eqref{eq:fcbf} for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, then for all initial conditions $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0$, the induced trace assignment $V$ satisfies $\psi$, i.e., $V \models \psi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:reachability}, for any $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0$, there exists a finite time $T$ such that $\mathbf{x}(T) \in \mathcal{C}$, which means  that $\phi_2$ eventually holds. Therefore, $V \models \psi$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Quadratic program for controller synthesis} \label{subsec:qp}

The controller synthesis conditions derived previously reduce to enforcing CBF and FCBF inequalities in the augmented state space. Let $b_1$ denote the CBF associated with safety specifications $\square \, \phi_1$ and $b_2$ denote the FCBF associated with liveness specifications ($\lozenge \, \phi_2$).

For $b_1$, forward invariance of the corresponding superlevel set is enforced via the condition in \eqref{eq:cbf}. For $b_2$, the FCBF condition in \eqref{eq:fcbf} ensures finite-time convergence to the superlevel set. Since the augmented system in \eqref{eq:augmented_system} is input-affine, all these conditions are affine in the augmented input $\bar{\mathbf{u}}$. By selecting a quadratic objective $\bar{\mathbf{u}}^\top \mathbf{W} \bar{\mathbf{u}}$, with $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{(m \cdot p) \times (m \cdot p)}$ positive-definite, the synthesis problem can be formulated as a convex QP:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:qp}
    \begin{aligned}
        \bar{\mathbf{u}}^\star = \min_{\bar{\mathbf{u}} \in \bar{\mathbb{U}}} \ & \bar{\mathbf{u}}^\top \mathbf{W} \, \bar{\mathbf{u}} \\
        \text{s.t.} \quad 
        &L_{\bar{\mathbf{f}}} b_1(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) + L_{\bar{\mathbf{g}}} b_1(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \bar{\mathbf{u}} \ge \! -\alpha \big(b_1(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\big),\\
        &L_{\bar{\mathbf{f}}} b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) + L_{\bar{\mathbf{g}}} b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \bar{\mathbf{u}} \ge \! -\gamma \operatorname{sign} \big( b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \big) \big| b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \big|^\rho,
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma > 0$, $\rho \in [0, 1)$, and $\alpha$ is a class-$\mathcal{K}$ functions. The closed-loop system is obtained by applying the feedback law $\bar{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \bar{\mathbf{u}}^\star(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$ in a sampled-data manner.
\begin{remark} \label{rem:quantifier}
    The results in Theorems~\ref{thm:safety_hyper} and \ref{thm:reachability_hyper} extend to $\exists^\star$, $\forall^\star \, \exists^\star$, and $\exists^\star \, \forall^\star$  hyperproperties by appropriately interpreting the quantifiers over initial conditions. In particular, for $\exists^\star$, it suffices that there exists $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0 \in \mathcal{C}$. For $\forall^\star \, \exists^\star$, for every $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\forall}$ there must exist $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\exists}$ such that $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0 = (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\forall}, \bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\exists}) \in \mathcal{C}$, while for $\exists^\star \, \forall^\star$, there must exist $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\exists}$ such that for every $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\forall}$, we have $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0 = (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\exists}, \bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\forall}) \in \mathcal{C}$.
\end{remark}
\begin{theorem} \label{thm:qp}
    Consider the augmented system in \eqref{eq:augmented_system} under the feedback controller obtained from \eqref{eq:qp}, and a HyperLTL formula $\psi$ of the form \eqref{eq:hyperltl_formula} belonging to $\forall^\star$, $\forall^\star \, \exists^\star$, $\exists^\star \, \forall^\star$, or $\exists^\star$.  Assume that the initial augmented state $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0$ is selected according to the quantifier structure of $\psi$ so that $\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0)$ is not violated, i.e., (i) for $\forall^\star$, this holds for all $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0$, (ii) for $\forall^\star \, \exists^\star$, for every $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\forall}$ there exists $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\exists}$, (iii) for $\exists^\star \, \forall^\star$, there exists $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\exists}$ for all $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0^{\forall}$, and (iv) for $\exists^\star$, there exists $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0$ such that $\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_0)$ is not violated.  If \eqref{eq:qp} is feasible for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, then the induced trace assignment $V$ satisfies $\psi$, i.e., $V \models \psi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    From Theorem~\ref{thm:safety_hyper}, satisfaction of the CBF constraints guarantees satisfaction of the specifications $\square \, \phi_1$.
    From Theorem~\ref{thm:reachability_hyper}, satisfaction of the FCBF constraint ensures that there exists a finite time $T$ such that $\bar{\mathbf{x}}(t) \in \mathcal{C}$, and hence the specifications $\lozenge \, \phi_2$ are satisfied. 
    Thus, $\varphi = \square \, \phi_1 \land \lozenge \, \phi_2$ holds along the closed-loop trajectories. The result for $\exists^\star$ and $\forall^\star \, \exists^\star$ follows by selecting initial conditions according to the quantifier structure of $\psi$ (see Remark~\ref{rem:quantifier}). Hence, $V \models \psi$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Grid sampling}

A lattice $P$ centered at $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with spacing $r > 0$ is
\[
    P(\mathbf{x}, r) = \Big\{ \mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x} + \sum_{i=1}^n a_i r \, \mathbf{e}_i, \;  a_i 
    \in \mathbb{Z} \Big\},
\]
where $\{\mathbf{e}_i\}_{i=1}^n$ is the canonical basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The associated $n$-dimensional cube centered at $\mathbf{y}$ of side length $r$ is
\[
    B(\mathbf{y}, r) = \Big\{ \mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{y} + \sum_{i=1}^n k_i r \, \mathbf{e}_i, \\ \; k_i 
    \in \left[ -\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2} \right] \Big\}.
\]
Consider an initial set $\mathbb{X}_0$ and a nominal initial condition $\boldsymbol{\xi} \in \mathbb{X}_0$ (see Fig.~\ref{fig:initial_grid}). Our goal is to compute an over-approximation of $\mathbb{X}_0$ using the grid-sampling algorithm of \cite{tan2022}. Specifically, for a given spacing $r$, a candidate lattice $P_{\text{cand}}$ is constructed around the center
$\big( \tfrac{\rho_{\mathbf{e}_1}^{\min} + \rho_{\mathbf{e}_1}^{\max}}{2}, \dots, \tfrac{\rho_{\mathbf{e}_n}^{\min} + \rho_{\mathbf{e}_n}^{\max}}{2} \big)$, where $\rho_{\mathbf{e}_i}^{\min} = \min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}_0}\mathbf{e}_i^\top \mathbf{x}$
and $\rho_{\mathbf{e}_i}^{\max} = \max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{X}_0}\mathbf{e}_i^\top \mathbf{x}$ for $i = 1, \dots, n$. The resulting point set is $P = \{\boldsymbol{\eta} \in P_{\text{cand}} \mid B(\boldsymbol{\eta},r)\cap\mathbb{X}_0 \neq \emptyset\}$.
Proposition~2 in \cite{tan2022} shows that $P = \{ \boldsymbol{\eta}_j \}_{j = 1}^N$ has finite cardinality and its associated $n$-cubes cover $\mathbb{X}_0$, i.e.,
$\mathbb{X}_0 \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^N B(\boldsymbol{\eta}_j, r)$.

\subsection{Control approach} 

For a fixed $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ and $\boldsymbol{\eta}_j \in P$, consider an alternative trajectory $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_j, \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j}(t)$ with initial condition $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_j, \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j}(0) = \boldsymbol{\eta}_j$ and input $\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j(t)$. The nominal trajectory is $\mathbf{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(t)$ with $\mathbf{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(0) = \boldsymbol{\xi}$ and input $\boldsymbol{\nu}(t)$. To satisfy \eqref{def:contraction} for this pair of $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ and $\boldsymbol{\eta}_j$ we must find corresponding nominal and alternative controllers $\boldsymbol{\nu}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j$, respectively. We define the state error $\mathbf{e}_j = \mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j$, with $\mathbf{e}_j(0) = \boldsymbol{\xi} - \boldsymbol{\eta}_j$.
\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:khalil}
\cite{khalil2002} Let $V:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ be a positive definite function of the error. Suppose there exist controllers $\boldsymbol{\nu}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j$, such that along the error dynamics, it holds that $\dot V(\mathbf{e}_j) \le -\alpha(V(\mathbf{e}_j))$, for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, where $\alpha$ is a locally Lipschitz class-$\mathcal{K}$ function. Then, there exists a class-$\mathcal{K} \mathcal{L}$ function $\zeta$ such that $\|\mathbf{e}_j(t)\| \le \zeta(\|\mathbf{e}_j(0)\|,t)$ for all $j = 1, \dots, N$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$.
\end{lemma}
Namely, if the controllers $\boldsymbol{\nu}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j$ satisfy the Lyapunov inequality $\dot V(\mathbf{e}_j) \le -\alpha(V(\mathbf{e}_j))$, then \eqref{eq:contraction} holds for the trajectory pair $(\mathbf{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\nu}}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_j,\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j})$. Define the augmented state and input $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j = (\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j)$ and $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j = (\mathbf{u}, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_j)$. Using the system dynamics in \eqref{eq:system}, the derivative of $V(\mathbf{e}_j)$ can be written as an affine function of $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j$. Consequently, the Lyapunov inequality can be expressed as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:lyapunov_ineq}
    -\left[ L_{\bar{\mathbf{f}}} V (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) + \alpha(V(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j)) \right] - L_{\bar{\mathbf{g}}} V (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \, \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j \ge \mathbf{0},  
\end{equation}
with $\bar{\mathbf{f}}$, $\bar{\mathbf{g}}$ defined in \eqref{eq:augmented_system}.

Writing \eqref{eq:lyapunov_ineq} for all pairs $(\mathbf{x}_{\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\nu}}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_j,\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_j})$, $j = 1, \dots, N$, yields a set of $N$ inequalities whose simultaneous satisfaction can be verified by solving
\begin{equation} \label{eq:qp2}
    \begin{aligned}
        c(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \max_{t, \bar{\mathbf{u}}} \quad & t \\[-5pt]
        \text{s.t.} \quad & \mathbf{q}_1(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) + \mathbf{Q}_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \, \bar{\mathbf{u}}
        \ge t \mathbf{1}, \\
        & \bar{\mathbf{u}} \in \mathbb{U}^{N+ 1},
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathbf{x}} = (\mathbf{x, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_N)$ and $\bar{\mathbf{u}} = (\mathbf{u, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_1}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_N)$, and
\[
    \mathbf{q}_1(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \begin{bmatrix} 
    -L_{\bar{\mathbf{f}}} V (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_1) - \alpha(V(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_1)) \\ \vdots \\ -L_{\bar{\mathbf{f}}} V (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_N) - \alpha(V(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_N)) 
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        &\mathbf{Q}_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \\[3pt]
        &\begin{bmatrix} 
        -L_{\mathbf{g}} V (\mathbf{x}) & \! \! \! -L_{\mathbf{g}} V (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1) & \! \! \! \mathbf{0}^\top & \! \! \! \dots & \! \! \! \mathbf{0}^\top \\ 
        -L_{\mathbf{g}} V (\mathbf{x}) & \! \! \! \mathbf{0}^\top & \! \! \! -L_{\mathbf{g}} V (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_2) & \! \! \! \dots & \! \! \! \mathbf{0}^\top \\ \vdots & \! \! \! \vdots & \! \! \! \ddots & \! \! \! \ddots & \! \! \! \vdots \\ -L_{\mathbf{g}} V (\mathbf{x}) & \! \! \! \mathbf{0}^\top & \! \! \! \dots & \! \! \! \dots & \! \! \! -L_{\mathbf{g}} V (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_N)
        \end{bmatrix}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
If $\mathbb{U}$ is polytopic, \eqref{eq:qp2} is a linear program. The optimal value $c(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$ represents the largest robustness margin with respect to violation of the $N$ Lyapunov inequalities at $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$.

Since $\mathbf{f}$, $\mathbf{g}$ and $\alpha$ are locally Lipschitz, and $V$ is continuously differentiable, $\mathbf{q}_1$ and $\mathbf{Q}_2$ are locally Lipschitz in $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$. For a fixed $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ and fixed $\mathbf{x}$, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i$, for all $i \neq j$, we study how the $j^\text{th}$ inequality changes when $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j$ varies within $B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j, r)$. Note that the $j^\text{th}$ inequality depends only on $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j$ and the inputs $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j = (\mathbf{u}, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_j)$. Suppose $q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) = -L_{\bar{\mathbf{f}}} V (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) - \alpha(V(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j))$ and $\mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) = -\big( L_{\bar{\mathbf{g}}} V (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top$ are Lipschitz in $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j$ on a compact set containing $B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j, r)$. Then there exist constants $L_{j, 1}$, $L_{j, 2} \ge 0$ such that for all $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j' \in B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j, r)$
\[
    |q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) - q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j')| \le L_{j,1} \|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j'\|_\infty,
\]
\[
    ||\mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) - \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j')||_\infty \le L_{j,2} \|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j'\|_\infty,
\]
Thus, if $c(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) > 0$, Lipschitz continuity guarantees the existence of a neighborhood of $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$ in which $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j$ may vary while $q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j \le 0$ remains satisfied. 

\begin{proposition}
    Let $(c(\bar{\mathbf{x}}), \bar{\mathbf{u}}^\star(\bar{\mathbf{x}}))$ be the optimal solution of \eqref{eq:qp2} at $\bar{\mathbf{x}}$. For a fixed $j \in \{1,\dots,N\}$ and fixed $\mathbf{x}$, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i$, for all $i \neq j$,  define $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star = (\mathbf{u}^\star, \hat{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star)$, i.e., the components of $\bar{\mathbf{u}}^\star$ that appear in the $j^\text{th}$ inequality, and
    \[
        \rho_j := \frac{2 \, c(\bar{\mathbf{x}})} {L_{j,1}+L_{j,2}\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star\|_\infty}.
    \]
    If $\rho_j \ge r$, for any $\bar{\mathbf{x}}' = (\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{j-1}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j', \mathbf{x}_{j+1}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_N)$ with $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j' \in B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j,r)$, the $j^\text{th}$ inequality remains satisfied, i.e., $q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star \ge 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:qp2}, we get $q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star \ge c(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \mathbf{1}$. For any $\bar{\mathbf{x}}' = (\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{j-1}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j', \mathbf{x}_{j+1}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{x}}_N)$ with $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j' \in B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j,r)$,
\[
    q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}^\star
\]
\begin{equation} \label{eq:x'}
    \ge \big( q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') - q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big) + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') - \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star +  c(\bar{\mathbf{x}}).
\end{equation}
Since $\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j'\|_\infty \le \tfrac{r}{2}$ for any $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j$, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j' \in B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j, r)$, we have
\[
    || \big( q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') - q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big) + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') - \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star ||_\infty
\] \[
    \leq || q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') - q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) ||_\infty + || \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j') - \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) ||_\infty \,||\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star ||_\infty
\] \[
    \leq L_{j, 1} \|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j'\|_\infty + L_{j, 2} \|\hat{\mathbf{x}}_j - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_j'\|_\infty  \,||\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star ||_\infty
\] 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:x'2}
    \leq (L_{j, 1} + L_{j, 2} \,||\bar{\mathbf{u}}^\star ||_\infty) \, \frac{r}{2} \leq (L_{j, 1} + L_{j, 2} \,||\bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star ||_\infty) \, \frac{\rho_j}{2} \leq c(\bar{\mathbf{x}}),
\end{equation}
if $\rho_j \ge r$. From \eqref{eq:x'} and \eqref{eq:x'2}, $q_1^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) + \big( \mathbf{q}_2^j(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j) \big)^\top \bar{\mathbf{u}}_j^\star \ge 0$, which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Examples of hyperproperties}

\subsection{Approximate opacity}

Consider the system in \eqref{eq:system}. Its output is $\mathbf{y} = h(\mathbf{x})$, where $\mathbf{h}: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^q$ is continuously differentiable. The objective is to prevent a passive intruder that observes only its output, to infer whether the system trajectory has visited a prescribed secret set
\begin{equation} \label{eq:secret_set}
    \mathbb{X}_s = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X} \mid b_s(\mathbf{x}) < 0 \}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition} \label{def:security}
    Consider a control system as in \eqref{eq:system}, an initial condition $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{X}_0$, and $\delta \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$. The system is \emph{$\delta$-approximate infinite-horizon opaque} if there exist an initial condition $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 \in \mathbb{X}_0$ and controllers $\boldsymbol{\nu}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}: \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \rightarrow \mathbb{U}$ such that the corresponding trajectories $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(t)$, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0,\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(t)$ satisfy
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $\| h(\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(t)) - h(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0, \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(t)) \| \le \delta$, for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$,
        \item $\mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\nu}}(t) \in \mathbb{X}_s \Rightarrow \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0, \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}(t) \notin \mathbb{X}_s$, for all $t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{definition}
These conditions ensure that whenever the real trajectory visits the secret set, there exists an indistinguishable (with respect to the measurement precision $\delta$ of the intruder) alternative trajectory that is non-secret at the same time. Consequently, the intruder cannot determine whether the secret set has been visited. We assume $\mathbb{X}_0 \not\subseteq \mathbb{X}_s$, since otherwise opacity is trivially violated.
\begin{remark}
    Infinite-horizon opacity is the continuous-time analogue of infinite-step opacity in discrete-time systems.
\end{remark}
The above property can be expressed as the following HyperLTL specification:
\begin{equation*}
    \psi_{\text{op}} = \forall \tau \, \exists \hat{\tau}. \, \square \Big( \big( \|h(\tau) - h(\hat{\tau})\| \le \delta \big) \, \land \, \big( \neg s(\tau) \, \lor \, \neg s(\hat{\tau}) \big) \Big),
\end{equation*}
where $s(\tau)$ denotes that the trajectory $\tau$ lies in $\mathbb{X}_s$. We encode opacity in the augmented state space $\bar{\mathbb{X}} = \mathbb{X} \times \mathbb{X}$ with $\bar{\mathbf{x}} = (\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}) \in \bar{\mathbb{X}}$. Similarly, we have $\bar{\mathbb{U}} = \mathbb{U} \times \mathbb{U}$ with $\bar{\mathbf{u}} = (\mathbf{u}, \hat{\mathbf{u}}) \in \bar{\mathbb{U}}$. 

We define $\mathcal{C}_1 := \left\{ (\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}) \in \bar{\mathbb{X}} \mid || h(\mathbf{x}) - h(\hat{\mathbf{x}})|| \leq \delta \right\}$ and $\mathcal{C}_2 = \left\{ (\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}) \in \bar{\mathbb{X}} \mid \big( \mathbf{x} \notin \mathbb{X}_s \big) \lor \big( \hat{\mathbf{x}} \notin \mathbb{X}_s \big) \right\}$. The secure set is given by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:secure_set}
    \mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_1 \ \cap \ \mathcal{C}_2.
\end{equation} 
By Theorem~\ref{thm:safety_hyper} and Remark~\ref{rem:quantifier}, forward invariance of $\mathcal{C}$ implies satisfaction of $\psi_\text{op}$, and hence $\delta$-approximate infinite-horizon opacity.
\begin{remark}
    The proposed formulation provides a continuous-time notion of approximate opacity together with an online control synthesis method. In contrast to existing works that consider discrete-time opacity \cite{yin2021, anand2024, zhong2025}, the proposed approach enables real-time enforcement via CBF-based QPs.
\end{remark}

To enforce forward invariance of $\mathcal{C}$, we construct smooth CBFs for $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$. The condition $(\mathbf{x},\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \in \mathcal{C}_1$ 
is equivalent to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:delta_cbf}
    b_1(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \delta^2 - ||h(\mathbf{x}) - h(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) ||^2 \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Given that $h$ is continuously differentiable, $b_1$ is continuously differentiable everywhere. Using \eqref{eq:secret_set}, the condition $(\mathbf{x},\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \in \mathcal{C}_2$ 
is equivalent to 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:max}
    b_s(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0 \, \text{ or } \, b_s(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0 \, \Leftrightarrow \, \max \{ b_s(\mathbf{x}), b_s(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \} \ge 0.
\end{equation}
We define the smooth under-approximation of the $\max$ operator as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:lse_cbf}
    b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) := \frac{1}{\beta} \ln \left( e^{\beta \, b_s(\mathbf{x})} + e^{\beta \, b_s(\hat{\mathbf{x}})} \right) - \frac{\ln 2}{\beta}, \quad \beta > 0.
\end{equation}

Assume that the secret set $\mathbb{X}_s$ is an ellipsoid defined by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:ellipsoid}
    b_s(\mathbf{x}) := (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_s)^\top \mathbf{Q}_s (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_s) - 1 < 0.
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{c}_s \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and $\mathbf{Q}_s \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ symmetric positive-definite.

The corresponding gradients are
\[
    \frac{\partial b_1}{\partial \bar{\mathbf{x}}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    -2 \left(\frac{\partial h(\mathbf{x})}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\right)^\top \mathbf{e}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \\
    2 \left(\frac{\partial h(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}}\right)^\top \mathbf{e}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
    \end{bmatrix},
    \quad
    \frac{\partial b_2}{\partial \bar{\mathbf{x}}}
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
    2 \,w_{\mathbf{x}} \, \mathbf{Q}_s (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_s) \\
    2 \, w_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \, \mathbf{Q}_s (\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{c}_s)
    \end{bmatrix},
\]
where $\mathbf{e}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = h(\mathbf{x}) - h(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ is the output error, and
\[
    w_{\mathbf{x}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \frac{e^{\beta \, b_s(\mathbf{x})}}{e^{\beta \, b_s(\mathbf{x})} + e^{\beta \, b_s(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}}, \quad
    w_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = 1 - w_{\mathbf{x}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}).
\]

In many applications the secret set $\mathbb{X}_s$ may consist of multiple disjoint regions, i.e., $\mathbb{X}_s = \bigcup_{k=1}^K \mathbb{X}_s^{(k)}$, where $\mathbb{X}_s^{(k)} = \big\{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{X} \mid b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) < 0 \big\}$. For each $\mathbb{X}_s^{(k)}$, we have $b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) = (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_k)^\top \mathbf{Q}_k (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_k) - 1$, with $\mathbf{c}_k \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathbf{Q}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. The condition that a trajectory is non-secret set becomes $\min_k b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) \ge 0$. To ensure that at least one of the real or alternative trajectories is non-secret, we impose 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:max_min}
    \max\!\left\{ \min_{k} b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}),\; \min_{k} b_s^{(k)}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \right\} \ge 0
\end{equation}
and define smooth under-approximations as follows:
\begin{equation*}
    m(\mathbf{x}) := -\frac{1}{\beta'} \log\!\left( \sum_{k=1}^k e^{-\beta' b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x})} \right) \le \min_{k} b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}), \quad \beta' > 0.
\end{equation*}
Similarly, we have $m(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \le \min_{k} b_s^{(k)}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$. Next, 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:cbf_multiple_secret}
    b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \frac{1}{\beta}
    \log\!\left(
    e^{\beta m(\mathbf{x})} + e^{\beta m(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}
    \right)
    - \frac{\log 2}{\beta}, \quad \beta > 0.
\end{equation}
We have $b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \le \max\{m(\mathbf{x}), m(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\}$, and hence enforcing $b_2(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \ge 0$ guarantees \eqref{eq:max_min}. The gradient of $b_2$ is given by
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\partial b_2}{\partial \bar{\mathbf{x}}} =
    \begin{bmatrix} 2 \,w_\mathbf{x}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \sum_{k=1}^K p_k(\mathbf{x})\, \mathbf{Q}_k (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}_k) \\[2.5mm] 2 \, w_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) \sum_{k=1}^K p_k(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\, \mathbf{Q}_k (\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{c}_k) \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
where $w_\mathbf{x}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = \frac{e^{\beta m(\mathbf{x})}}{e^{\beta m(\mathbf{x})} + e^{\beta m(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}}$, $w_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}) = 1 - w_\mathbf{x}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})$, and
\begin{equation*}
    p_i(\mathbf{x}) =
    \frac{e^{-\beta' b_s^{(k)}(\mathbf{x})}}{\sum_{j=1}^k e^{-\beta' b_s^{(j)}(\mathbf{x})}}, \quad k = 1, \dots, K.
\end{equation*}
Enforcing together the CBF conditions in \eqref{eq:cbf} for $b_1$ and $b_2$ via the QP in \eqref{eq:qp}, guarantees forward invariance of $\mathcal{C}$.

\section{Case Study} \label{sec:case_study}

In this section, we demonstrate the proposed framework on a robot operating in a 2D workspace. The objective is to simultaneously enforce a reach-avoid specification and $\delta$-approximate infinite-horizon opacity using the CBF-based QP developed in Section~\ref{subsec:qp}.

We consider a single-integrator
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{u}, \quad \mathbf{u} = \{ \mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{U} \mid -2 \leq u_i \leq 2\},
\end{equation}
with state $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2)^\top$ and input $\mathbf{u} = (u_1, u_2)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2$. The trajectories start from the initial set, i.e., $\mathbf{x}(0) = \mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{X}_0$. The system output is given by $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{h}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}$, i.e., the intruder observes the position directly, up to precision $\delta$.

The workspace contains two unsafe regions (obstacles) $\mathbb{X}_u^{(1)}$ and $\mathbb{X}_u^{(2)}$, modeled as ellipsoids (cf. \eqref{eq:ellipsoid}) with centers $\mathbf{c}_u^{(1)} = (1, 1.5)^\top$, $\mathbf{c}_u^{(2)} = (3.4, 4.6)^\top$, and matrices $\mathbf{Q}_u^{(1)} = \operatorname{diag}(4, 1)$, $\mathbf{Q}_u^{(2)} = \operatorname{diag}(4, 4)$. Similarly, the three secret regions are described by
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        &\mathbf{c}_s^{(1)} = (5.5, 3.9)^\top, \, \mathbf{c}_s^{(2)} = (1.8, 3.5)^\top, \, \mathbf{c}_s^{(3)} = (4, 6.2)^\top, \\
        &\mathbf{Q}_s^{(1)} = \operatorname{diag}(\tfrac{4}{9}, \, \tfrac{4}{9}), \mathbf{Q}_s^{(2)} = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1), \, \mathbf{Q}_s^{(3)} = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1)
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Finally, for the goal region $\mathbb{X}_g$, we have
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbf{c}_g = (5.2, 7.1)^\top \text{ and } \mathbf{Q}_g = \operatorname{diag}(100, 100).
\end{equation*}

The objective is twofold: (i) the trajectory must reach the goal region $\mathbb{X}_g$ while avoiding unsafe regions $\mathbb{X}_u^{(1)}$, $\mathbb{X}_u^{(2)}$, and (ii) the trajectory must be $\delta$-approximate infinite-horizon opaque with respect to the secret set $\mathbb{X}_s = \bigcup_{i=1}^{3} \mathbb{X}_s^{(i)}$.

We set $\delta = 0.2$, that represents the measurement precision of the intruder. Thus, whenever the real trajectory enters any of the secret regions, there must exist an alternative trajectory whose output remains within distance $\delta$ of the real output, while remaining outside the secret set at the same time.

Opacity is enforced in the augmented state space using two CBFs. The first one enforces $\delta$-indistinghuisability (cf. \eqref{eq:delta_cbf}) and the second one guarantees that either the real or the alternative trajectory is always outside of any secret region (\eqref{eq:cbf_multiple_secret}). Safety with respect to the two obstacles is enforced via CBFs, one for each obstacle.

The QP as in \eqref{eq:qp} is solved online in sampled-data fashion. Starting from initial condition $\mathbf{x}_0 = (0, 0)^\top$ and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 = (0, 0.1)^\top$ for the real and the alternative trajectory, respectively, the optimization-based controller drives the system toward the goal region while satisfying safety and opacity along the path. The resulting trajectories are presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}. 
\begin{figure}[tb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[scale=0.5]{fig/si0.2traj.pdf}
    \caption{Real and alternative trajectories for $\delta=0.2$}
    \label{fig:traj}
\end{figure}

This case study illustrates that the proposed CBF-based framework can simultaneously enforce reach-avoid specifications and continuous-time opacity constraints in a unified QP formulation.
